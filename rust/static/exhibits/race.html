BASIS

The ceiling is a pair, not one number. 1.63 TB/s is what this card sustains on a read stream (BabelStream 5.0 Dot 1630.4 / 1628.4 GB/s; matched-shape CUDA stream-read 1626.7 / 1626.3 at 16 GiB) and 1.792 TB/s is the datasheet: 512-bit × 28 Gbps GDDR7. Every pin uses the same numerator — 19.26 GB resident-active bytes per token for qwen3.8-27B at 4-bit (19.104 weights + 0.151 recurrent state + 0.009 fp8 KV at depth 256) — divided by that engine's measured decode ms/token at depth 256. Because each engine holds the weights in its own format (ours NVFP4-class, llama.cpp Q4_K_M, vLLM FP8), a shared numerator makes this a decode-time comparison drawn in bandwidth units, not a per-engine utilisation. vLLM's decode rows are still being measured; its pin is the llama.cpp decode position scaled by the measured prefill ratio (2404 / 2569) and drawn dashed until real rows land.

NOTHING HERE PASSES THE WALL

MTP speculative decode emits more than one accepted token per weight pass, so dividing the whole single-token weight pool by the per-accepted-token time gives 1.566 TB/s. That is 96% of the sustained ceiling, and at or above 95% this project reads a bandwidth number as an accounting error rather than a result: the device did not move 19.26 GB for that token, it moved that much for the round. The MTP pin is therefore drawn dashed as a counterfactual — the honest claim is 16.9 → 12.3 ms/token, a 1.37× speedup bought with redundant compute, not extra bandwidth. You don't break physics; you stop needing as much of it.
