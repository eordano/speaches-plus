# Laguna XS 2.1 on the wgpu backend

Poolside Laguna XS 2.1 -- a 33B-total / ~2.8B-active sparse MoE code model --
decodes and serves through `nv-models::laguna_wgpu`. The binary's `wgpu` feature
turns on `nv-models/laguna-wip`, `classify_wgpu_model` has a Laguna arm, and a
real `poolside/Laguna-XS-2.1-NVFP4` checkpoint answers a chat request over HTTP.
The measured serving figure lives in
[`05.1-wgpu-status.md`](05.1-wgpu-status.md); this page is the shape of the port
and the traps in it.

## 1. What Laguna is, and the three things that made the port work

Shape, all read from `config.json` and not restated here beyond what the port
turns on: 40 layers, hidden 2048, vocab 100352 with `tie_word_embeddings: false`,
GQA at `head_dim` 128 / `num_key_value_heads` 8, full-attention layers at
0,4,8,...,36 and sliding (`sliding_window` 512) at the other 30, 256 experts top-8
at `moe_intermediate_size` 512 plus an **ungated** 512 shared expert, a dense
`intermediate_size` 8192 layer 0 (`mlp_only_layers: [0]`), routing by sigmoid +
`e_score_correction_bias` with `norm_topk_prob: true` and
`moe_routed_scaling_factor` 2.5, `max_position_embeddings` 262144, eos `[2, 24]`.

Three features are the engineering content of the port:

- **Variable heads per layer** -- **48 on full-attention layers, 64 on sliding**.
  No other wgpu graph in this tree varies `n_q` per layer.
- **A softplus per-head output gate**, one scalar per head through a separate
  `g_proj`, where Qwen fuses its gate into `q_proj` and applies `sigmoid`.
- **Two RoPE regimes in one model**: full-attention layers use YaRN at θ=5e5,
  factor 32, `partial_rotary_factor` 0.5 and `attention_factor`
  1.3465735902799727; sliding layers use the default at θ=1e4 and partial 1.0 --
  with a different partial-rotary pairing from the one `gemma4_wgpu` implements
  (§3).

Quantization covers **only** the expert and shared-expert FFN tensors
(`compressed-tensors`, `nvfp4-pack-quantized`, group 16, fp8_e4m3 scales).
Attention, layer-0's dense MLP, the routers and `lm_head` stay BF16 -- which is
why the artifact is 21.6 GB and not ~9 GB, and why attention dominates the
per-token byte budget at ~71%.

The suffix set (`weight_packed` / `weight_scale` / `weight_global_scale` /
`input_global_scale`) is byte-for-byte what `qwen3_5_moe_wgpu::load_nvfp4` reads,
so `nv-weights` needs no change. Tensor-name deltas from Qwen: no
`model.language_model.*` prefix; `g_proj` is separate; `e_score_correction_bias`
is F32 `[256]`; no `mlp.shared_expert_gate`; and **no `+1` norm convention** --
Laguna uses plain RMSNorm, so `load_norm_plus_one` must not be used.
**`module_pattern_matches` (`nv-weights`) does not understand compressed-tensors'
`re:` prefix**, which Laguna's `ignore` list uses throughout: detect quantization
by presence-of-`weight_packed`, never by the ignore list.

## 2. Kernels reused verbatim

| kernel | why it is safe as-is |
|---|---|
| `rmsnorm_bf16`, `rmsnorm_residual_bf16` | Laguna uses plain `RmsNorm::new`; do **not** apply Qwen's `load_norm_plus_one` convention |
| embed row gather (`gather_rows_bf16`) | Laguna does not scale embeddings by √H (gemma4 does) |
| `silu_mul` (SwiGLU, bf16 round-trip on the SiLU) | matches `laguna.rs` exactly |
| argmax stage1/stage2 | Laguna has **no** final logit softcapping; do not wire the softcap variant |
| bf16 GEMV family | all Laguna `n` are even |
| `quantize_nvfp4_bf16` / `push_quant_rows` with `use_sel` | per-slot quantization against per-expert `input_global_scale` already works |
| `flash_splitk_stage1_bf16kv` + `stage2` | `group = n_heads / n_kv` is a **runtime uniform**, so GQA groups 6 and 8 share one pipeline; `start`/`total`/`window`/`ring` are uniforms too -- **variable heads-per-layer costs zero kernel work** |

Dense layer-0 (`mlp_only_layers: [0]`) reuses the shared-expert sub-path verbatim
with `shared_inter -> intermediate_size` -- zero new kernels. fp8 KV, int8/fp8
`lm_head` and W8/fp8 attention projections all have wgpu analogues wired into
`gemma4_wgpu`; they are liftable, not writable. **Explicitly do not wire**
`moe_permute.wgsl`, `moe_grouped_gemm.wgsl` or `moe_unpermute_scatter.wgsl` --
they exist for M>1 and at bs=1 are strictly worse than the slot-parallel
formulation (§4).

The genuinely Laguna-specific WGSL is small: a softplus per-head gate
(`lgw_attn_gate`, stable `max(x,0) + log(1+exp(-|x|))` form with the gate rounded
to bf16 *before* the multiply); the router top-k (`lgw_moe_router_topk`: sigmoid
-> `+ e_score_correction_bias` -> top-8 on the **biased** score with weights from
the **unbiased** score -> renormalise -> x2.5); the MoE combine (the shared expert
is ungated, so Qwen's `sigmoid(shared_logit)` is correctly dropped and
`routed_scaling` added); and `rot_scale` in norm+rope for the YaRN
`attention_factor`. Variable heads-per-layer is handled by uniform-buffer
parameterisation, not pipeline specialisation and not padding to max, so all
layers collapse onto one pipeline per kernel and head count only moves the
dispatch grid; the KV cache is unaffected (`num_key_value_heads` is a scalar 8)
and shared scratch is sized by `max(NQ)=64`.

## 3. The correctness gate

The bar is the one `qwen3_5_moe_wgpu` was held to: tiny random weights, GPU argmax
== CPU reference every step, worst relative logit error reported, state
carry/reset exact.

**The oracle is order-replicating, not merely mathematically equivalent.** That is
what makes an "exact" claim mean anything, and it is the reusable repo-wide
policy: `ref_rmsnorm` replicates `rmsnorm.wgsl::rms_reduce`'s warp tree and its
8-term warp-leader combination; `ref_gemv_bf16` replicates the 128-lane strided
accumulation plus its binary tree reduction (stride 64->1);
`ref_rmsnorm_residual` takes the sum of squares from the **unrounded** f32 sum and
only then stores the bf16-rounded residual, exactly as `rmsnorm_residual.wgsl`
does. A merely-equivalent oracle produces apparent divergences that are oracle
artifacts, and this port has a recorded case of exactly that.

The full-graph gate (`laguna_wgpu_model.rs`) asserts `worst_rel` within
`6.0 * BF16_ULP` normalised by logit scale, requires argmax agreement only where
the top-2 gap is decisive, and carries **mutation tests**
(`oracle_is_sensitive_to_a_perturbed_lm_head`,
`oracle_is_sensitive_to_a_perturbed_middle_layer`) that prove the oracle can
actually fail. Per-block gates measure exact (0.000e0) agreement on attention
(full and sliding), gating and the dense MLP. **It runs on synthetic weights
only**: tensor-naming, scale-swizzle and NVFP4 alpha bugs all survive a
synthetic-weight test and appear only against real files, so the synthetic gate is
necessary and never sufficient.

**`gemma4_wgpu`'s partial-rope is wrong for Laguna**, and it is worth pinning with
a test. `rope_tables` implements partial rotary by *zeroing* `inv_freq` above
`partial*head_dim/2`, pairing `(i, i+head_dim/2)`; Laguna pairs
`(d, d+rotary_dim/2)` inside `[0, rotary_dim)` and passes the tail through
unrotated. Different rotations whenever `rotary_dim < head_dim`, i.e. on all 10
full-attention layers, and silently wrong numbers if the gemma4 table is used.
`qwen3_5_moe_wgpu::ar_rope_at` already implements the exact split.

## 4. The expert gather is already optimal -- do not redesign it

Both `qwen3_5_moe_wgpu` and `laguna_wgpu/gpu.rs` dispatch expert GEMVs as
`(grid.x, grid.y, slots)` over a contiguous `[E, N, K]` NVFP4 stack, resolving one
4-byte `sel[slot]` load into a base-plus-`expert_id·stride` address. That is
structurally what llama.cpp's `kernel_mul_mv_id` and MLX's `gather_qmv` do;
contiguous layout versus indexed gather is a false dichotomy, and every fast stack
does both together.

- **Do not materialise a permutation.** llama.cpp sorts only at >=32 tokens, MLX
  at >=64 (token, expert) pairs; bs=1 top-8 is far below both. Each selected
  expert is one contiguous 512 KB region, so sorting cannot improve coalescing or
  TLB behaviour, and it costs an argsort plus a scatter -- two more full pipeline
  barriers.
- **The dependent read chain does not stall; the barrier around it does.** The
  `sel[slot]` load is one L2-resident cacheline against megabytes of subsequent
  streaming, while wgpu inserts a full barrier between every pair of dispatches in
  a compute pass. Reduce barriers between routing and the first weight read; do
  not restructure the gather.
- **The index must never leave the GPU.** No readback, no host-side routing, no
  CPU-visible sync point -- that is what preserves the single-submit whole-step
  graph (one encoder, one `begin_compute_pass`, one `queue.submit`).

Where room remains is dispatch granularity: at 256 experts / top-8 / inter-512 the
per-dispatch payload is 4.72 MB, small enough relative to per-dispatch cost that
the MoE block is latency-bound on a wide GPU. The two moves that fatten it are
conversion work, not gather redesign -- append the shared expert as expert index
256 (its shape is exactly that of one routed expert) and stack gate and up into
one tensor. **The slot-256 fold reassociates the additions and is therefore not
bit-exact**: gate it at 1 bf16 ULP plus greedy agreement, extend `input_globals`
to 257 entries, and keep the existing
`experts_gate.input_globals == experts_up.input_globals` assert intact.

## 5. The live defect, and the order to fix things in

**Attention decode is a naive per-head scan** dispatched at `grid1(n_q, 1)`
(`lgw_attn_decode`) -- a handful of workgroups on a many-core GPU, on the
full-attention layers, which are exactly the layers with unbounded KV -- instead
of the in-tree split-K flash decode, which needs no kernel change to adopt (§2).
**Any Laguna kernel-level timing taken before this is fixed is a strawman.**

Then quantize attention. One inherited conclusion must be rejected explicitly: the
CUDA roadmap found `W8`/`ATTN_FP8` net *slower* under the fused-QKV default, but
that is a latency-bound artefact of a compute-saturated step. The wgpu path is
bandwidth-bound at a small fraction of roofline, so the regime is inverted and
attention is the largest single lever in the byte budget. Pair it with q|k|v|g
fusion: quantizing attention shrinks its GEMVs enough to re-enter the
bad-granularity regime on its own.
